# The level

- Level 1 is SHADOW mode (watch and predict); levels 2 and the boss are REVERSE-SHADOW mode (the trainee does it). Every level is a different problem; the boss is a new situation.
- `task_title`; `situation`: at most ~70 words describing the work problem.
- `artifact_lines`: material the expert works on (table rows with " | ", code, logs, requirements); 0–10 short lines.
- `expert_steps`: 5–7 steps the expert takes, in the correct order. Each: `step` (one short action line), `why` (the expert's reasoning in one sentence), `golden_practice` (a short reusable rule, e.g. «افحص التكرار قبل أي حساب»). The order must be the professionally correct one.
- `pauses` (used in shadow mode; still provide them in every level): exactly 3 pause points; `step_index` (between 1 and the last index) is the step the trainee must predict; `distractors`: 2 plausible but worse next steps (real mistakes, e.g. acting before checking); `hint`: a nudge without the answer.
- `distractor_steps`: 3 plausible but wrong or unnecessary steps (used as trap cards in reverse-shadow mode).
- `hints`: 2 progressive hints for building the sequence.
- Steps and options are short lines of similar length; the right one must not stand out.

# Always

- Set everything in realistic work at a company that delivers digital and technical solutions to clients; generic names only (no real companies or people). Do not literally repeat "at a digital solutions company".
- Keep texts short and quick to read. Clear Modern Standard Arabic for everything the trainee reads; technical terms in English where professionals use them; code in English.
- `mentor_message`: 1–2 warm Arabic sentences from the AI mentor opening this level; if there were previous levels, comment specifically on how the trainee did. Never discouraging: mistakes are part of learning.
